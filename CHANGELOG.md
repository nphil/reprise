# Changelog

## Unreleased

- An optional local AI check for intros and credits Reprise could not confirm. Off by default (a switch on the Library page, with the address and model, default `qwen3-vl-4b` on your llama-swap server). After an analysis it shows a few small frames of the episode to a model that is already loaded and, where the frames show credits, offers the span as a "Local model suggestion" with every frame's verdict as evidence; it never loads a model, skips quietly when the server is busy or down, and never changes what Reprise found or sends anything without your approval. `tools/reprise_vision_eval.py` scores it read only against the exact skips you set: on 10 Apothecary Diaries credits it proposed all 10 and ended 9 exactly at the file end (one 24 s too long), but started a median 31 s late (safe direction, 2 of 10 within 2 s), with no story frame called credits; the one intro it was tried on was 60 s short, so treat it as advice.
- Reprise now suggests the intro and credits skip from the video file's own chapter marks. A file with a chapter titled Opening, Intro or OP proposes the intro, and one titled Ending, Credits, Outro or ED proposes the credits, from its start to the end of the file when only previews follow (The Apothecary Diaries S02E01: 22:15 to the end). It is a suggestion you approve ("Suggested by the file's own chapter marks"), never sent by itself; only a stretch that comparing episodes also confirms becomes an ordinary finding. When most of a show's episodes have the chapter at one usual length, an episode whose chapter is a different length is flagged in its notes.
- Analysis is about twice as fast on the CPU side, with identical results, and a full graphics card no longer switches the GPU off. Reprise had lost the GPU picture reduction on the live job: the shared P40 was out of memory three times in a row, and three failures withdrew the reduction until restart, so every frame came to the CPU whole (The Apothecary Diaries S01E15: 7,467 CPU s instead of 3,075). A reduction or plain decode that fails for lack of video memory is no longer counted against the GPU, repeated other failures pause only that codec for ten minutes, and a startup check cut short by a full card is run again after ten minutes. Each stretch of sound is also decoded once per analysis and every read inside it is served the same samples its own decode would give (sound decodes per episode 144 to 16, 26% less CPU on S01E15; Community S01E05 60% less, The Office S05E12 55% less). The drafts (every range with its evidence and verdict) were identical before and after for Apothecary S01E15, Community S01E05 and The Office S05E12 (see [DESIGN](DESIGN.md)).
- Sending to Plex no longer makes Reprise's own results stale, and a library refresh no longer takes back an exact skip you set. After a full refresh of the TV library 96 episodes read "Plex changed" (The Apothecary Diaries 55, Community 18, Invincible 10 and others), and the exact skips you had set on the Apothecary episodes had fallen back to "proposed", with their overrides set aside. The cause was Reprise's own sending: Plex's intro and credits markers were saved with each episode as part of its binding to Plex, so once Reprise had written a marker the next refresh saw a different binding and started the episode over (86 of the 87 episodes sent before that refresh were marked). The check that an episode is still compared with the same neighbours counted the same fields, so a marker change on a neighbour would have made it "Comparison changed" too. A binding now counts only what identifies the episode: its file and part, library, folder mapping, show, season and episode. The markers, titles and the address Plex was read at are still kept up to date, and never start an episode over. When a binding does change, the exact skip you set keeps its approval and a locked range keeps its override (the file is the same file), and an episode that was only compared with the changed one reads "Comparison changed" instead of being set back. Undoing a send now works after a refresh as well. At start-up Reprise approves again the exact skips that were set back (it does not touch Plex) and stamps the comparisons it made earlier so results that were current stay current; an episode that already reads "Plex changed" needs one new analysis (see the [playback API guide](docs/reprise-api.md)).
- You can now set the exact intro or credits skip for an episode yourself. Reprise's own finding was the only way to get a skip, so an episode where it found nothing, found the wrong place or could not confirm it (the Plex report's `UNIQUE`, `EARLY`, `LATE` and unconfirmed rows) could only be left as it was or take Plex's marker on trust. The review's new **Set a skip exactly** button takes a type and a start and end time (each can be filled from the player) and saves one approved, locked skip (`origin: "owner"`, reason "Set by you") after a deliberate yes (`acknowledge_owner_span: true`, as a suggestion needs). It replaces every other skip of its kind: Reprise's own findings, its suggestions and Plex's marker for that kind are no longer used, sending it writes it in their place (or says there is nothing to publish when it is exactly what Plex already has, and undo puts Plex's marker back), and the Plex report shows `SET_BY_YOU` ("Set by you: the intro skip (1:00 to 2:20)") instead of EARLY, LATE or unconfirmed. It may start before, end after or bridge what Reprise could not confirm; only a scene you protected or a skip you locked stops it, plus the old checks (inside the file, not the last two seconds, a current analysis, the audio track). A new analysis keeps it, and automatic mode never sends or replaces it: an episode with one is left to you (see the [playback API guide](docs/reprise-api.md#setting-a-skip-exactly)).
- Automatic mode: Reprise now handles intro and credits by itself. It analyses new and changed files (Sonarr and Radarr tell it, and a look at Plex every 30 minutes catches the rest), works through the rest of the library quietly at low priority, sends only sure matches (confirmed by picture and sound) to Plex after a daily backup of Plex's database, and sends phone alerts when something needs attention; three switches under Settings, Sharing control it, and films get credits suggestions only (see [DESIGN](DESIGN.md) and the [README](README.md#automatic-mode)).
- Analysis now does most of its picture work on the NVIDIA GPU, with identical results. The P40 shrinks every decoded frame to the comparison grid (`scale_cuda`, exact 2 x 2 averages) so about an eighth of the data comes back, the CPU finishes the canonical picture from those whole-number sums, and the frame pairs of a window are measured in batches by kernels of our own (`cuda_kernels/compare.cu`, shipped as PTX and loaded through the driver; no CUDA toolkit and nothing of NVIDIA's in the image). On one Apothecary Diaries pair (the 20-second intro window, runs alternated on a busy host) an analysis took 70 and 72 CPU seconds instead of 130 and 138 and about 40 s of wall time instead of 62 s, and confirmed the same range (793 to 21,932 ms) every time; the ffprobe audit, which stays on the CPU as the independent check on the GPU's decode, is now 59% of what is left. Every GPU step is held to the CPU (startup self-tests, a check of every shrunk frame against FFmpeg's checksums, a CPU re-measure of any pair within 0.001% of a limit, a CPU repeat after any GPU error) and no result changes, so the rule set stays `native-signal-review-first-v9`. The card is shared politely: a new setting (Library page "GPU memory limit", `gpu_vram_cap_mb`, default 2 GB) caps the video memory, which is never more than what is free less 1 GB, is held only while an analysis runs and is given back when it ends. `REPRISE_GPU_REDUCE=0` and `REPRISE_CUDA_COMPARE=0` turn the two parts off.
- An analysis now carries on after a restart instead of ending as "Job failed". Restarting the server (or updating Reprise) used to leave the job failed and its remaining episodes undone: a 54-episode The Apothecary Diaries analysis died at 29% (15 episodes done, one cut off, 38 never started), because the old "Restart recovery" setting never applied to Reprise jobs and nothing kept the list of episodes still to do. The list now lives in the saved job, so at start-up every unfinished analysis or library refresh resumes by itself, about two minutes after the app is up and only when Plex and your media folders can be reached (it waits up to 30 minutes, and unavailable media never counts as an attempt): episodes already finished are skipped, only the one that was being read is redone, and the Library and Activity panel say "Resumed after a restart" (the job carries a `recovery` note: `waiting`, `resumed` or `paused`). It cannot loop without doing useful work: an episode cut off twice is set aside with "Skipped after 2 interrupted attempts — analyze it again to retry" and the job moves on; three automatic resumes within 15 minutes that finish no episode pause the job with "Analysis paused: it kept stopping. Resume when ready." and the Resume button; an episode Reprise cannot read (file missing or changed, unsupported, refused) is recorded once and never retried by a resume, and no longer fails the whole job (it fails only when no episode produced a result); a job you cancelled stays cancelled. An update or stop (SIGTERM) marks the episode being read as interrupted, never failed, saves no half-finished result and does not count toward the two attempts; an interrupted library refresh simply runs once more. Two settings (no page control yet): `reprise_auto_resume` (default `true`) and `reprise_resume_delay_seconds` (default `120`); see [DESIGN](DESIGN.md) and the [API guide](docs/reprise-api.md).
- Re-analysing an episode no longer leaves two overlapping versions of an intro you had locked, and the Plex report tells apart what it knows from what it could not confirm. A locked range now wins over the new run: its pieces of the same kind are cut back to what lies outside the lock (Community S03E01 showed `3:41-3:42` twice, `3:42-3:44` twice and so on, which the report read as a false EARLY or LATE), and results already stored that way are repaired once at start-up (approvals fall back to proposed only on a piece that had to change; locks, manual ranges and reviewed keeps are untouched). The report no longer calls every keep a "protected scene": only a keep you made, locked or reviewed is (`CUTS_KEEP`, "a scene you protected"); one the analysis made means only that comparing episodes could not confirm it, shown as the lower-key `COVERS_UNCONFIRMED` ("covers 9 s Reprise could not confirm"). An intro or credits that none of at least 3 other episodes shares is now said to be one of a kind and not skipped (`UNIQUE`, with the number of episodes compared, in the report, the text form and the episode's reasons), kept apart from "could not compare" and "partly matched"; analyses from before need a new one to show it, except where the old note "no repeating intro or credits" already says it.
- An intro that only some episodes share, or whose sound sits a little further from its picture, is now confirmed. A Community season 3 run confirmed an intro in 11 of 22 episodes and left the other 11 as suggestions; five of those were Reprise's doing. (1) It looked for where the sound lines up only within a quarter of a second of the picture, but every release has its own sound/picture sync: across 104 pairs of that season's Blu-ray rips the sound sat from 413 ms early to 372 ms late, and a fifth of the pairs were beyond a quarter of a second (E09's two good matches were 251 and 268 ms out). It now looks within a second. (2) It compared an episode only with its three nearest, but E08 and E12 share a sound mix with no other episode (7th and 8th nearest of each other), E13 shares its intro with E15 (4th nearest) and E19's nearest three held a file since replaced. An intro or credits suggestion (Plex's or online) that the nearest three leave less than half confirmed is now also compared with up to six more episodes, three at a time, stopping once half is confirmed; a window the nearest three confirm, and the opening and ending fallbacks, cost nothing more. Nothing is looser: a range is still confirmed only where picture and sound are identical over the same time, so a wrong guess can only lose a confirmation. Six episodes correctly stay unconfirmed because their intro really differs (E05 and E11: their own picture; E10: its own sound; E14, E17 and E20: no standard intro in what was scanned). The verification profile is v9 (it was v8), so every earlier result is stale and is analyzed again on its next run.
- One episode whose file went missing or changed no longer stops an analysis of the rest. A 22-episode Community season 3 analysis ended `failed` ("Media became unavailable or stale") at episode 18, whose file had been deleted mid-run for a re-download, and the other episodes were never analyzed. Reprise now notes that episode (its Library row shows the usual "File missing" or "File changed" with the Refresh library button, and its result says why), carries on with the next one, and finishes `completed` with a warning that names each left-out episode (`Community S03E18 Basic Lupine Urology`) and why, in the Library Activity panel like a refresh's skipped episodes. A file that vanishes while it is being compared is treated the same. Real failures (a crash, Plex unreachable) still fail the job, and Cancel still cancels it.
- Episodes are now compared whatever format their files are in. Reprise used to compare two files only when picture and sound were stored the same way, so a season that mixes releases lost comparisons: Community S03E18 (an H.264 8-bit web copy with E-AC-3 sound among HEVC 10-bit Blu-ray rips with AC-3) could not be compared with any neighbour. Every file is now brought to one common form first: the picture to the same small grid and brightness scale whatever the codec, bit depth or resolution (1080p against 720p or 4K of the same shape, and copies with a few columns trimmed, which are lined up from the pictures themselves); the sound to one mono signal at one sample rate whatever the codec or number of channels; and a copy sped up from 24 to 25 frames a second is read at its own speed. The limits were measured again on real files: over about 10,300 frame pairs of the same footage in two different files the picture stayed under 0.9 of its limits in 99 % of frames, while different footage was at least 2.6 times over them (another scene 22 times), and a single odd frame in a fade no longer breaks a long match while any cut to different footage still does. Sound differences are now judged against how loud the shared sound is, which is what lets a Blu-ray and a web copy of The Office agree over 19.5 of 20.2 seconds of their title music instead of 7.7 seconds in six pieces. On the real files S03E18 now completes against episodes 17, 19 and 16 and confirms the 1.4-second end logo it shares with episode 19 (it has no title sequence, and everything else in its last 90 seconds is kept), and S03E02 confirms 23.0 seconds of its title sequence as before. Still not compared, so never confirmed: black bars burned into one file only, HDR against SDR, releases graded to a different brightness, a speed change whose sound was pitch-corrected, and interlaced or VC-1 video. The graphics card still decodes; the comparison itself now always runs on the processor. The verification profile is v8.
- A **Plex report**: for one show or season, one read now says where the skip buttons Plex already has differ from what Reprise found, instead of piecing it together from Plex and Reprise by hand. `GET /api/reprise/report?show=<title>&season=<n>&format=json|text` (owner only, read only, nothing written to Plex) gives one row per episode with Plex's markers next to Reprise's skips, keeps and suggestions, and plain flags: Plex skips into a scene Reprise protects (with the seconds), starts too early or ends too late (with the seconds, differences of 1 second or less ignored), has no skip Reprise found, or the episode is unconfirmed, not analyzed, failed, out of date or already sent to Plex, plus a headline such as "3 episodes where Plex skips into a scene Reprise protects". `format=text` is a table under 4 KB. The Library has a **Plex report** button for the same thing as a page (`/reprise/report`), and a row opens that episode's review.
- Fixed Community episodes whose analysis failed with "This episode can't be compared exactly with the others, so nothing could be confirmed" and the detail "Native audio decoder did not cover the window" (four episodes of season 3: E06, E09, E11 and E22). Plex ends a credits marker at the very end of the file, but the sound of a Blu-ray rip usually stops 15 to 40 milliseconds before its picture, and Reprise refused to compare any window the sound did not completely cover. It now compares a window that runs past the end of a sound up to where that sound ends and claims nothing beyond it. A window that starts after the sound has ended, a gap in the sound, a damaged file and a decoder error still stop the comparison, as before.
- One episode in another picture or sound format no longer costs the others a comparison. Reprise used to pick an episode's three nearest neighbours first and then drop any it could not compare exactly, so on Community season 3 episodes 17, 19 and 20 were compared with two others (episode 18 is H.264 8-bit, the rest HEVC 10-bit) and episode 18 with none. It now goes down the season nearest first, skips an episode it cannot compare exactly and takes the next nearest until it has three. The result says how many were left out and why ("1 other episode of this season has a different picture format, so it was left out of the comparison."), and an episode whose own format differs from every other one is told so in one sentence instead of "None of the other episodes of this season could be used for the comparison". An episode taken in place of a nearer one is not asked about at the online intro databases.
- Sending to Plex can now replace just one kind of marker. Approving only credits (for example a wrong Plex credits marker on Community S03E02 and S03E07) replaces Plex's credits and leaves Plex's intro exactly as it is, and approving only the intro does the same the other way round; before, Reprise refused with "Existing markers of an unreviewed type cannot be replaced or removed". Plex's other kind keeps its marker row and its part data untouched (its `index` only moves when Plex's numbering needs it), Undo restores everything exactly, and the prepared before/after list marks each marker that stays as Plex has it with "unchanged, kept as Plex has it". Markers Reprise cannot read safely still stop the change.
- Fixed Community analyses that ended with "This episode can't be compared exactly with the others, so nothing could be confirmed" (reason "Native frame metadata failed"), and an episode that showed Analyzing with no job running. The first was the container running out of processes, not a bad file: Docker caps Reprise at 128 processes and threads, one FFmpeg decode used 37 of them (55 on the graphics card, 21 for sound) because only its decoder was limited to one thread while the raw-picture output behind it started one thread per CPU of the host, and three other episodes compared at once went past the cap. FFmpeg then could not start a thread and logged `[rawvideo] [error] ff_frame_thread_encoder_init failed` (processor) or `[swscaler] [error] Failed initializing scaling graph (Resource temporarily unavailable)` (graphics card), and the exact reader rightly refuses any decode that logs an error. Every decode now limits its output to one thread too (6 threads on the processor, 9 on the graphics card, 6 for sound), and the number of episodes compared at once follows what the container's process limit has left (2 in a 128-process container, 3 where there is no limit). Nothing was made more lenient: a warning or error from FFmpeg still fails the analysis, and the failure now names FFmpeg's own line instead of only "Native frame metadata failed". The stuck Analyzing row was an analysis the container was stopped in the middle of, which nothing ever ended. Reprise now keeps track of the analyses it is carrying out, and at startup and whenever an episode's status is read it ends one that nothing is carrying out as Cancelled ("Analysis was interrupted before it finished"), so it can be analyzed again. Tests replay the two real FFmpeg lines and a record left running by a stop.
- Fixed analyses that sometimes failed with "Analysis stopped because this file can't be compared exactly" and the reason "Native frame payload stalled", on a busy machine. While Reprise waited for a frame's picture it stopped reading FFmpeg's progress log; FFmpeg then stopped to write that log, so neither side moved until the 30-second guard ended the analysis (found with FFmpeg 5.1 and 8.1; a host whose pipes hold only 8 KB makes it likely). The log is now always read, whatever the comparison is doing. A decoder that really stops still fails after 30 seconds, and one that gets more than 1,024 frames ahead of the comparison still fails the analysis. A test replays the decoder's log first and its pictures after, which stalled every time before the fix.
- A guided **Setup guide** for new installs, with install docs that take someone from nothing to a working Reprise. After you create your account Reprise opens a seven-step checklist: Connect Plex, Choose libraries and check your media folders, and Refresh the library are required; a graphics card, online intro lookup, Sonarr and Radarr, and Send results to Plex are optional. Each step shows from your real settings whether it is done, says why in plain words (for a media folder it names the folder Plex uses, where Reprise looks for it and which mount to add; for sending it names the exact Plex folder that is not shared, using the same check as the Allow sending to Plex switch) and has one button that opens the place where you do it, including Connections' Edit dialog on the right tab (`/servers?edit=<id>&tab=paths`) and a Library refresh (`/reprise?refresh=1`). It stays under Setup guide in the sidebar and in Settings, Advanced, Account; Skip for now stops it opening by itself, and an install that already has an account and a connected server never sees it open on its own and has nothing reset. `GET /api/reprise/setup-guide` (owner only; no Plex call, no catalogue walk, nothing written to Plex's database) returns the steps and `POST` saves "skipped" or "finished". Also changed on the way: the Sharing switch's refusal now reaches the page in plain words and names the missing Plex folder (it was hidden behind "Invalid request or operation"), saving a Connections dialog no longer fails because of a Plex config folder the wizard filled in and nothing is mounted at, the sidebar's Plex-safety chip works before any server is connected, and the first-run tour now opens by itself on the Library only (it used to open on whichever page you reached first, which could cover a dialog the guide had just opened). README: new Install and set up (Unraid template, Docker Compose, every setting explained, sharing media folders), Turn on sending to Plex (back up with Plex SQLite `.backup`, the same host path Plex uses, read-write `/plex`, `PLEX_CONFIG_FOLDER=/plex`, restart only Reprise) and Troubleshooting sections; the Unraid template and Compose example gain clearly labelled optional fields (a second media folder, the Plex config folder and `PLEX_CONFIG_FOLDER`, none active by default, so a fresh install stays review only).
- A library refresh that could not read a few episodes no longer ends as a red "Failed". It finishes as completed with a plain warning, and the Library Activity panel lists each episode (`Show S03E08 Title`) with why it was skipped (for example "Plex and FFprobe can't read its audio/video streams", or "the file on disk isn't the size Plex has recorded"); the job's `warning` and `refresh.skipped_items` carry the same. Real failures (Plex unreachable, a crash) and scans of specific files still fail. Library health shows the Previews and Chapters columns as off ("Preview thumbnails are off for this server", with the Change in Connections link) for a server whose preview thumbnails switch is off, instead of counting files "to do".
- The GPU cards no longer show a red "This FFmpeg has no Vulkan: report it on GitHub" for a build that leaves Vulkan out on purpose. This image's FFmpeg is built without it (only Dolby Vision profile 5 preview thumbnails need it), and the app now reads the build's own recipe (`configure-args.txt`) to tell: it shows a grey "Vulkan: not included in this build" note, sends no warning notification and logs one plain INFO line at startup instead of the startup warning. A build whose recipe includes Vulkan, or an FFmpeg with no recipe, keeps every error and warning it had.

The Settings tabs now all fit on a tablet (the current tab no longer scrolls the first ones out of sight; narrower windows scroll with a fade), the sidebar's Plex-safety note is a tooltip when the sidebar is collapsed, notification details are a real button, and every (i) help button has a 24 px target. The server list has one name everywhere, "Connections" (Settings, then Connections), instead of "Servers page" or "Media Servers"; "What's New" reads Reprise's own releases instead of the upstream project's.
- Review-only setups see no false alarms. With preview thumbnails off (and chapter thumbnails, Intro & Credits and loudness off) Reprise no longer requires or checks the Plex config folder (`plex_config_folder_needed`); a wrong or read-only folder shows a muted note instead of a red error; and the Connections card says "Previews off" instead of "Previews on". A server Reprise cannot reach now says why in plain words and what to check, with the technical message behind Details, on the card, in the edit dialog and in the add flow.

- A Library row that is not current now says why instead of always saying "File changed", and Refresh clears the rows it should. Live, 34 of 4,258 rows read "File changed" (Andor S1E2 among them) although nobody changed a file: Sonarr had upgraded those episodes (old file deleted, new one imported) and Plex had dropped the old media, so the old row's file was simply gone. The rows now read File missing (renamed, replaced or removed), File changed (edited in place), File unavailable, Media folder unavailable (storage not mounted yet), Library settings changed, Plex changed, Analyze again (Reprise's comparison method was updated, which is what every profile revision does to analyzed episodes) or Comparison changed, each with a sentence and the button that helps (`stale` on each item and on the open episode: `code`, `label`, `message`, `next`, `action` "refresh" or "analyze"); the lookup answer for clients is unchanged. Nothing could clear those rows: a refresh that could not read even one file (the 6:22 PM one: "2 skipped") skipped the clean-up for the whole library, and a scan of one new file never cleaned up at all. A refresh now removes the entries Plex no longer lists and keeps only those of the episodes it could not read, and a scan of just the new file (a Sonarr or Radarr webhook) also drops that episode's old row.
- Library page layout and usability fixes found on a 1280×720 tablet. The episode list no longer collapses to a sliver (rows overlapping, the help text on top of the first row) when Activity, finished jobs or ticked rows share the pane: the list is a scroller with guaranteed room, Activity is capped and scrolls under its own head, the selection bar is one row, and nothing spills out of the pane. Below 1100 px the list stacks above the review. New: Select all, a "Review next" shortcut, "Season N" filter labels, a status line that stays in view and can be dismissed, "Play this section" scrolls the player into view, a file the browser cannot play no longer leaves an empty grey player, and the changed-file chip, sentence and next step follow the service's `stale` reason. A running job's percent no longer repeats on every episode of a multi-episode job.
- Analysis is about 3.6 times faster with the same results (Community S03E02 against its 3 nearest episodes, 50 min to 14 min on the busy NAS, every stage's output identical): a frame pair that cannot match stops at the first limit it exceeds instead of computing all seven; the spatial-information check uses exact integer sums; the ffprobe audit of a window runs beside the decode instead of before it (a failed audit still fails the scan, at its end); up to 3 references are searched at once (`REPRISE_ANALYSIS_WORKERS`, 1 to 8; results are merged in reference order, so they do not depend on which finishes first); with an NVIDIA GPU the frame-pair limits are first checked on it (`media_preview_generator/reprise/cuda_kernels/`: our own kernels shipped as PTX and loaded through the driver's `libcuda`, no CUDA toolkit in the image; a plane is decided on the GPU only when the CPU's float arithmetic is certain to give the same verdict, anything inside the error band is redone on the CPU, and the kernels are self-tested against the CPU on first use; `REPRISE_CUDA_VERDICT=0` turns it off); and the frames of a window scanned for one episode are kept per file generation (`reprise-scan-cache.db` in the config folder, 256 MB at most, least recently used out first) so the episodes that compare with it do not decode them again (a season scans 2.4 times fewer seconds of video). The Intel iGPU is not used to spread references (`REPRISE_SPREAD_DECODERS=1` tries it): its decode itself costs almost no CPU, but copying 10-bit frames back from it costs about as much as decoding on the CPU, so it measured 10 to 30 % slower than all references on the P40.
- The progress bar of an analysis moves from the start: the target's scan and every reference's scan report as they go (it used to stay at 0 % until the first reference was finished, minutes on a busy host).
- Reprise now uses online intro and credits times (IntroDB, SkipDB, and TheIntroDB when it has a key; the sources already under Settings > Intro & Credits) in two ways. As hints, they say where to search in the other episodes of the season: a hint only adds places to look, and the strict sound and picture confirmation decides exactly as before, so with no online data a result and its speed are unchanged. As suggestions, a time that comparing episodes could not confirm is stored as a proposed skip with `origin: "online"`, `reason_code: "online_suggestion"` and the label "Suggested by IntroDB — not confirmed by Reprise." (several databases read "A and B"); it is never eligible to skip until the owner approves that exact range. When Plex and a database agree within 5 seconds at both ends it is one range with `origin: "plex"` and the label "Suggested by Plex and IntroDB — not confirmed by Reprise."; an online time that overlaps a Plex marker of the same kind without agreeing is never added. Every suggestion now carries `suggested_by` (for example `["Plex", "IntroDB"]`; empty on other ranges, and on a Plex suggestion saved before this change, which is Plex's).
